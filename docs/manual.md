# Co-Owner's Manual
For co-owners.

## Body structure
Two A-pillars, two B-pillars, two C-pillars, two D-pillars. It's asaaab.

A: Take care of the people putting in the work. | Oxygen mask first. Self care.
B: Everyone's paid the same. | Everyone takes home the same split of profits per hour invested.
C: If you want more growth from asaaab and it's not more growth from the current thing, invest in your people. People are what made Saab great, people will make asaaab thrive. | It sustains the people it employs well enough they can take on their own whimsy endeavours, using the resources they are a co-owner in, whether it be content or shark tanking their idea to the board.
D: A break even model. Capped growth potential and contentment. Good enough. | Equal work for equal pay.
ROOF: There is always a why.

## Warning lights
! Something almost always broken.
You still need to show up and put in work. Not everyone will love this workplace. Something almost always broken.
Instead of complaining, making a plan to fix it will be praised. Actually.
! Letting off the clutch.
Anything new here starts in the clutch phase: jerky, loud, full of stalls. That's expected. Don't learn stick in bumper to bumper traffic. We build the empty parking lot first, so you can stall without wrecking anything.
The safety net: every change has a backup, nothing gets deleted, every step can be undone. A stall is information, not damage.
Before you say "ok, I'm ready for more," step back and look at what you made. Check that it's reasonable from the other person's side. Mortar dries, so catch it while it's wet.
Making room for people to stall is whimsymaxxing.

## Specifications

- Growth: capped growth potential and contentment
- Pay: equal work, equal pay
